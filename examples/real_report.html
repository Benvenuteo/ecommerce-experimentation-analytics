Executive summary

Randomized Hillstrom experiment: the following estimates compare each campaign arm with the no-email control. They are intention-to-treat contrasts.

  • Mens E-Mail: 1.25% vs 0.57% in the control group; estimated conversion lift +0.6805 pp (unadjusted 95% CI [+0.5014, +0.8641] pp; Holm-adjusted p <0.0001).
  • Womens E-Mail: 0.88% vs 0.57% in the control group; estimated conversion lift +0.3111 pp (unadjusted 95% CI [+0.1502, +0.4745] pp; Holm-adjusted p 1.57e-04).

The largest point estimate of conversion lift versus control was for Mens E-Mail (+0.6805 pp). This is a comparison of point estimates; the report does not include a direct statistical test between the two treatment arms.

Randomization check: the sample-ratio-mismatch chi-square test returned p = 0.9037. This provides no evidence of an allocation-ratio mismatch; it does not prove that every aspect of the experiment was flawless.

Commercial limitation: incremental spend is not profit or ROI. Campaign costs and product margins are not available.

02 / Randomized experiment (Hillstrom)

The Hillstrom randomization supports intention-to-treat contrasts between each email campaign and the no-email control under the original experiment design. The primary endpoint is conversion within two weeks. Two-sided p-values for the two primary comparisons are adjusted using the Holm method. The displayed 95% Newcombe intervals are unadjusted; bootstrap intervals for incremental spend per assigned customer are exploratory. Campaign delivery costs and product margins are unavailable, so profit and ROI cannot be inferred.

03 / Interpretation and guardrails

Business decision rule: before rolling out a campaign, assess adjusted significance, effect size, uncertainty, incremental spend and the unknown delivery and merchandise costs. A positive point estimate alone is not sufficient evidence of commercial gain.

Comparison scope: each treatment is compared with the no-email control. The report does not perform a direct statistical comparison between Mens E-Mail and Womens E-Mail.

Separation of datasets: REES46 customer identities and Hillstrom customer identities are unrelated and are never joined. Observational event analytics cannot establish the causal effect of emails. Experimental estimates cannot be transferred automatically to another marketplace or era.

Historical context: Hillstrom is a 2008 experiment. The REES46 event window is shown above. Neither dataset represents current customers of any other business.
